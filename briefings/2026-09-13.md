## Literature scan

Checked against all 72 filenames in `docs/literature/` and against the standing suggestions carried in the 07-10 through 09-06 briefings (Arrigoni Battaia 2016, Bennett 2026, Carr 2025, Cerdosino 2025, Claeyssens 2022, Davis 2023, Dong 2024, Feldmeier 2013, Firestone 2026, Gagnon-Hartman 2026, Gallego 2021, Gronke 2015, Hall 2026, Infante-Sainz 2020, Kikuchihara 2022, Kikuta 2023 (HSC), Laferte-Urrutia 2026, Laishram 2026, Li 2025, Li & Zheng 2025, Liu 2024, Lujan Niemeyer 2026, Matsuda 2012, Mirza Khanlari 2025, Roy 2026, Runnholm 2023, Sebastian 2026, Smirnov 2026, Verhamme 2018, Weiss 2024, Wu 2020, Xue 2017, Yu 2025, Zhang 2024 (MAMMOTH), Zhang 2026 (LAMOST), Zhang & Zaritsky 2024 — none re-listed). **Deliberately weighted instrumental this week**, because the week's work was a PSF calibration and three of the four items below bear directly on decisions the spec leaves open; that also means only one is 2025-or-later, which is the honest trade.

**Recent:**

1. **Weiss 2025** — Using Lyman Alpha Absorption to Measure the Intensity and Variability of z~2.4 Ultraviolet Background Light (arXiv:2504.13253, April 2025). Uses Lyα *absorption* against z~2–3 galaxies across the HETDEX database to measure a mean UV background of 12.9 ± 3.7 nW m⁻² sr⁻¹ at a median observed 4134 Å, and — the part that matters here — finds the flux varies significantly with the galaxy density of the field. This is the same instrument and the same wavelength regime attacking the *diffuse background* problem the 08-31 `VDFI Notes.txt` derivation concluded VDFI cannot access in emission: the note's argument is that a diffuse Lyα background is smooth in the observed frame and therefore removed by construction from both the galaxy stack and the random stack, leaving ⟨I⟩·b irreducibly degenerate. Weiss get at ⟨I⟩ through absorption instead, which is exactly the complementary route — and their density dependence is a direct empirical handle on the *b* half of that product. Note this is a different paper from the Weiss 2024 already on the standing list, and different again from the Weiss et al. 2021 f_esc work `GOALS.md` §6 leans on.

**Older, likely not yet reviewed:**

2. **Liu 2022** — A Method To Characterize the Wide-Angle Point Spread Function of Astronomical Images (arXiv:2110.11598, November 2021; ApJ 925, 219). Forward-models the entire image as a scene — every bright star's scattered light *plus* the sky, simultaneously, in a Bayesian framework (the `elderflower` package) — on the argument that at low surface brightness the scattered light and the "true" sky are not separable, so the classical measure-annuli-then-subtract-sky recipe biases the profile either flat or steep depending on field geometry. Two direct contacts with `psf-model.md`: it is the standard reference for the **aureole** question §6.1 leaves open (a `moffat+pl` form, i.e. whether a single Moffat is the right profile at all, with the literature's wide-angle power-law indices running n ≈ 1.6–3), and its central claim — that sky and PSF wings must be fit together — is the sharpest available critique of §1's decision to use the galaxies' own background annulus. That decision is defensible here for a different reason than Liu's (the point is to recover the operator the stack experiences, annulus included, not an abstract PSF), and that distinction is worth stating in the appendix rather than leaving a referee to raise it.

3. **Stephan 2024** — Atmosphyre: Modelling Atmospheric Chromatic Dispersion for Multi-Object Spectrographs (arXiv:2407.14356, July 2024; SPIE 13096). A Python package for characterizing atmospheric-dispersion flux losses in fiber MOS, with the operational recommendation that the guiding wavelength should sit **bluer than the band midpoint, roughly 25–45% of the way through**. `psf-model.md` §5.2 recovers λ_ref = 4484 ± 123 Å from the DAR fit and reads it as the guider/ADC reference — on a 3500–5500 Å band, 4484 Å is 49% through, which is close to but slightly redder than Stephan's recommended range. That is either a real check on the fit or a mild tension worth understanding, and it is the kind of independent, instrument-side corroboration that turns §5.2's λ_ref agreement from a coincidence into an argument. Also directly relevant to the spec's **open item 3** (residual ADR centroid shift vs λ), which the DAR model now partly explains but has not closed.

4. **Guo 2023** — On the evolution of the size of Lyman alpha haloes across cosmic time: no change in the circumgalactic gas distribution when probed by line emission (arXiv:2304.13057, April 2023; MNRAS 522, 4275). Listed here not as a discovery — `GOALS.md` §4 item 5 already cites the result — but because **the paper itself is not in `docs/literature/`**, which holds Guo 2024 twice and this not at all, and recommendation 2 makes it the single most important external comparison the project has. It reports no size evolution across a wide redshift baseline; VDFI is about to re-measure its own z~2/z~3 split with a kernel that is 12% wider in kpc at low z. Reproducing a null and manufacturing one look identical unless the PSF is handled, so this is the paper the redshift result will be read against, and it should be on disk before the comparison is made rather than after.
