## What's next

**1. Close criterion 1c. The `[60,100]` bin is the bin that contains R_vir, and that is not a coincidence you get to shrug at.**

The gap is specific and it is aimed at the exact place Paper I is most exposed. `GOALS.md` §4 states the headline in one line — "the headline is the transition, not the kinematics alone" — and the transition is located at R_vir. Criterion 1c was pre-registered precisely as the test that could take that headline away, and it is currently failing, with an unexplained ~−5% velocity offset against truth in the bin spanning 60–100 kpc, reproduced across three configurations since 08-17. Every other bin behaves. The one that misbehaves is the one the paper's central claim lives in.

The concrete action is now available in a way it was not last week: `build_velocity_response_matrix` and `deconvolve_velocity` shipped on 08-20, and the truth generator was shown to be linear (1.16 km/s) with cond(M) = 45.1, which is what makes closed-form deconvolution legitimate rather than wishful. So run the response matrix specifically on `[60,100]` and ask the one question that decides this: **does the −5% survive deconvolution, or is it the PSF-plus-binning operator?** Those are opposite outcomes. If it's the operator, the offset is a known forward-modelling effect you correct and report, and 1c passes. If it survives, there is a real radius-dependent bias sitting exactly on the transition, and Paper I's Results section cannot be written until it is understood — not "should be caveated," cannot be written, because a −5% bias localized at R_vir is indistinguishable in the plots from a weakened version of the effect being claimed.

Worth being blunt about the failure mode here: the tempting move is to attribute the anomaly to the fiber-sampling effect already identified at level 1, note the agreement in direction, and move on. That attribution has not been demonstrated for this bin, only for the outermost one, and "a known effect points the same way" is exactly the reasoning a referee unpicks first. The deconvolution makes it a measurement instead of an argument, which is the whole reason the tool was built.

This unblocks: the Paper I Results section, and by extension the §7 re-run pass, which there is no point running while the velocity estimator's behaviour at the headline radius is unresolved.

**2. Apply the per-km/s → per-Å unit fix at the injector. It is documented in-code, it is understood, and the code says it is still not applied.**

`injection.py` currently carries its own diagnosis in comments at lines 2795–2796 and 2972–2973: the line profile has unit integral in *velocity* and is written into a *per-Å* cube, so injected flux is low by `c/λ_obs` — **72.5× at z = 2.4, and z-dependent** — followed by the words "fix NOT applied." There is a compensating multiplication inside `l_inj_for_luminosity` (line 2132, behind a flag), which is a workaround at the luminosity-sizing end, not a fix at the emission end. Any code path that builds a template without going through that hook is still 72.5× low.

The fix is a multiplication by `C_KMS / lam0_obs`. The reason it is recommendation 2 rather than 4 is not difficulty but contamination: it breaks criterion 1a — the one criterion the ledger currently records as **PASSED** — and it breaks the §2.4 luminosity bootstrap. A banked pass that was measured through a known 72.5× flux error is not banked. So: apply it, re-run 1a, and re-bank or un-bank it honestly. Note this is independent of recommendation 1 — the bug is in flux normalization and does not move a median centroid — so the two can proceed in either order or in parallel. Say so in the spec, because "does the unit bug affect the velocity criteria?" is a question that will otherwise get re-asked every time someone reads the ledger.

This unblocks: criteria 2–7, all of which are flux or luminosity criteria and none of which mean anything until the injected flux is the flux you asked for.

## Literature scan

Checked against all 66 filenames in `docs/literature/` and against the standing suggestions carried in the 07-10 through 08-16 briefings (Arrigoni Battaia 2016, Bennett 2026, Carr 2025, Cerdosino 2025, Claeyssens 2022, Davis 2023, Feldmeier 2013, Gagnon-Hartman 2026, Gallego 2021, Gronke 2015, Hall 2026, Kikuchihara 2022, Li 2025, Li & Zheng 2025, Lujan Niemeyer 2026, Matsuda 2012, Roy 2026, Runnholm 2023, Saldana-Lopez 2026, Sebastian 2026, Smirnov 2026, Verhamme 2018, Weiss 2024, Wu 2020, Xue 2017, Yu 2025, Zhang 2026 (LAMOST), Zhang & Zaritsky 2024 — none re-listed). Weighted toward the two things this week made urgent: **what the emission does at and beyond one virial radius**, and **who else has separated an inner from an outer domain observationally.**

**Recent:**

1. **Kozlova 2026** — Two domains of extended Lyman α emission around galaxies: from local radiation to environmental regulation (arXiv:2608.16665, August 2026). Uses the MUSE eXtremely Deep Field to measure individual Lyα halo surface-brightness profiles for 21 LAEs at 3<z<4 out to 50 kpc — more than two virial radii for a typical R_vir ≈ 20 kpc — and argues for **two distinct domains**, an inner one set by the galaxy's own radiation and an outer one regulated by environment. This is, at higher redshift and on individual objects rather than a stack, both of VDFI's first two theses at once: Paper I's transition near R_vir and Paper II's "the one-halo term tracks the galaxy, the two-halo term tracks the environment." Posted six days ago and it needs reading before it is cited by someone else as having got there first — the honest framing is that it is corroboration from an independent instrument, and that VDFI's absolute frame and cosmic-noon sample are what it does not have.

2. **Firestone 2026** — HETDEX: Star Formation Stochasticity Diagram of Lyman Alpha Emitting Galaxies at Cosmic Noon Confirms Three Archetypes (arXiv:2607.23062, July 2026). Reconstructs non-parametric Gaussian-process star formation histories with Dense Basis for 270 HETDEX LAEs in COSMOS using CANDELS rest-UV-through-NIR photometry, and confirms that the sample is well described by three SFH archetypes. Same instrument, same field, same ancillary catalog as VDFI, and it supplies an externally validated three-way SFH classification for exactly the burst → post-burst → steady ordering `GOALS.md` §6 pre-registers as a falsifiable sequence — meaning that split can be defined against a published scheme rather than invented in-house, which is worth a great deal when the prediction is directional.

**Older, likely not yet reviewed:**

3. **Kikuta 2023** — UV & Lyα halos of Lyα emitters across environments at z=2.84 (arXiv:2302.12848, ApJ 947, 75, February 2023). Stacks Subaru/HSC images of 3,490 LAEs to a Lyα surface-brightness sensitivity of ~1×10⁻²⁰ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² arcsec⁻², fits a **two-component exponential** and recovers scale lengths of 1.56 ± 0.01 and 10.4 ± 0.3 pkpc, then splits on Mpc-scale environment including a protocluster. It is the closest published analog of VDFI's own two-component fit and environment split, and its result — no clear environmental dependence outside protocluster cores, very large halos inside them — is a sharper, testable version of the Steidel 2011 null that `GOALS.md` §5 builds Paper II's tension around. ⚠️ **Filename collision:** `docs/literature/` already holds `Kikuta 2023 - Stacked Lya NB Around QSO and Fitting.pdf`, which is a different paper by the same author in the same year. Disambiguate on save.

4. **Zhang 2024** — MAMMOTH-Subaru III. Lyα Halo Extended to ~200 kpc Identified by Stacking ~3300 Lyα Emitters at z=2.2–2.3 (arXiv:2301.07358, ApJ 961, 63, 2024). Median-stacks ~3,300 spectroscopically vetted LAEs (8% contamination) across ~12 deg² of Subaru/HSC imaging and traces the halo to ~200 kpc at 10⁻²⁰ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² arcsec⁻². The relevance is the radial range and the redshift: it is a large-N detection of Lyα well beyond R_vir at essentially VDFI's redshift, from a survey selected on overdensity, which makes it the natural external amplitude comparison for the two-halo term now that the photon budget has ruled out in-situ case-B star formation as its origin. ⚠️ Note the existing `Zhang 2026 - Cross-Correlations between CLAMATO and Lya Forest in COSMOS.pdf` is unrelated.
