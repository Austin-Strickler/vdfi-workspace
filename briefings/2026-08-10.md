## What's next

**1. Settle the LSF convention before any cube injection, then push the ladder to the level that actually produces a sensitivity curve.**

The harness is at level 1 of 7. Levels 0 and 1 check the injector against the truth generator — two independent numerical paths agreeing to 0.64%, with three deliberate convention breaks confirmed to fail the check. That is real work and it caught two genuine bugs (the cell-8 Å-vs-km/s units error, which was manufacturing exactly the 70× failure it exists to detect; and the half-pixel centroid bias in `predict_binned_observables`, which read as a clean 2.5 km/s systemic shift at every radius — the worst possible disguise). But neither level touches real data, and neither produces the thing the outer null needs.

The blocker is already written down in the 08-07 fifth-pass entry and left open: **the injector applies no LSF, the truth generator does.** `build_source_template` emits `sigma_kms = 250`; cell 10 predicts 320 with `lsf_kms=200`. Harmless for the centroid (≤0.07 km/s, blend bin only — symmetric broadening doesn't move a median), which is why it hasn't bitten yet. It will bite at level 7, where the harness would inject a line narrower than the instrument can physically produce into spectra that are already LSF-convolved, and it makes every width/skew comparison off by the whole LSF — precisely effects (b) and (c) that §3.5 calls predictions rather than errors. Pick one convention, assert it in `check_level0` with a tolerance tight enough to fail (the same lesson as the 1.0 → 0.05 km/s fix), and move on. This is hours, not days, and it is much cheaper now than after a full cube run.

What it unblocks is the whole point: "consistent with zero out to 2700 kpc" is a **null**, and a null without a demonstrated sensitivity curve reads to a referee as non-detection-by-insensitivity. Paper I's outer claim, Paper II's two-halo thesis and Paper III's beyond-R_vir budget term all sit on it. One caveat to carry into the write-up: the 08-07 entry established that a hard `r_trunc` makes a knee steep enough (`dlnV/dlnr` = −215 at 367") that **the outermost bin's injected truth is systematically depleted by the truncation choice**. The comparison stays self-consistent because the truth generator sees the same knee, but the `[2000, 3000]` bin is not testing what a real halo would do there, and the sensitivity curve should say so rather than quote that bin as if it were.

## Literature scan

Checked against all 66 filenames in `docs/literature/` and against the standing suggestions carried in the 07-10 through 08-02 briefings (Arrigoni Battaia 2016, Carr 2025, Cerdosino 2025, Claeyssens 2022, Davis 2023, Gagnon-Hartman 2026, Gallego 2021, Hall 2026, Li 2025, Roy 2026, Runnholm 2023, Sebastian 2026, Smirnov 2026, Verhamme 2018, Weiss 2024, Wu 2020, Xue 2017, Yu 2025, Zhang 2026 (LAMOST), Zhang & Zaritsky 2024 — none re-listed). Weighted toward this week's two live problems: **defending a faint outer null with a demonstrated sensitivity**, and **the f_esc > 1 falsification of an in-situ two-halo origin.**

**Recent:**

1. **Saldana-Lopez 2026** — The Lyman-alpha and Continuum Origins Survey II: the connection between the escape of ionizing radiation and Lyman-alpha halos in star-forming galaxies (arXiv:2504.07074, ApJ, accepted January 2026). Lyα and UV continuum imaging of 42 nearby star-forming galaxies with direct LyC measurements spanning f_esc(LyC) = 0.01–0.49, finding extended Lyα ubiquitously and LyC emitters showing systematically *more compact* Lyα morphologies than non-leakers. This is the closest published thing to Paper III's redistribution-vs-loss test — compactness anticorrelating with escape is the low-z, LyC-anchored version of the Steidel f_esc–scale-length prediction — and it is the paper to check that prediction's exact form against before it goes in an abstract (`three-paper-program.md` §8 item 8).

2. **Bennett 2026** — The birth of the intracluster medium: the evolution of multiphase gas and Lyman-α haloes in a simulated z∼3 protocluster (arXiv:2603.16991, July 2026). A cosmological zoom-in of a massive cluster progenitor to z = 2.7 with enhanced CGM resolution, tracking how Lyα halo emission changes as accretion, mergers and feedback build the ICM. Directly relevant to the environment split (`GOALS.md` §4 item 3) and to what replaced in-situ star formation this week: it predicts how the extended Lyα budget should shift with local overdensity in the regime where the two-halo term is supposed to live, which is a simulation-side prediction the Chartab-based split can be pointed at rather than a generic "environment matters" statement.

**Older, likely not yet reviewed:**

3. **Feldmeier 2013** — Searching for Neutral Hydrogen Halos Around z ∼ 2.1 and z ∼ 3.1 Lyα Emitting Galaxies (arXiv:1301.0462, ApJ 776, 75, September 2013). Reports only marginal evidence for extended Lyα halos at z ∼ 3.1 and none at z ∼ 2.1, contradicting contemporaneous detections — and the reason is the reason to read it: after a systematic audit they find their own surface-brightness limits are **5–10× larger than Poisson background fluctuations predict**, and state plainly that these uncertainties are routinely underestimated in the literature. Two specific mechanisms named, large-scale PSF wings and flat-fielding systematics. This is the canonical published version of the argument in `bin-correlation-diagnostics.md`, it is the paper a referee will cite at an underestimated outer error bar, and it is thirteen years old and still unaddressed by most stacking work — which makes closing it a citable contribution rather than housekeeping.

4. **Matsuda 2012** — Diffuse Lyα haloes around Lyα emitters at z = 3.1: do dark matter distributions determine the Lyα spatial extents? (MNRAS 425, 878, 2012 — *arXiv ID not confirmed by this scan; search by title*). Stacks 130–864 LAEs at z = 3.1 in subsamples and asks directly whether halo extent tracks the underlying dark matter distribution, i.e. environment. Worth pulling because `GOALS.md` §5 frames Paper II's thesis partly against **Steidel 2011's null** on halo size vs. overdensity, and this is the other z ∼ 3 measurement of the same question from the same era with a different sample and a different answer — a two-paper disagreement in the literature that VDFI's environment split is positioned to adjudicate, and better cited as a live disagreement than as a single null.
