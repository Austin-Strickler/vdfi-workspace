## Specs digest

**One move, and it is a real design change rather than a status update.** `injection-recovery.md` (08-25) rewrote **§2.3 and §2.4**: injection positions and randoms are no longer drawn from the field box. The footprint is now an alpha shape built from the source positions (α set from `alpha_scale` × the median nearest-neighbour separation) **unioned** with a `buffer_arcsec` = 15″ disk per source — union rather than global dilation, so the buffer rounds the outer boundary without filling MOSDEF's inter-mask notches back in. Redshifts are drawn uniform-within-hump via `find_redshift_humps` / `sample_redshifts`, a deliberate deviation from v4's bootstrap, with `min_dz` now validated against the **summed hump width** rather than `z_hi − z_lo`. Both default to `None`, reproducing pre-v8 behaviour, so levels 0–6 are untouched.

The reasoning recorded there is the part worth keeping: for an amplitude result the zero point *is* the measurement, so a random dropped in sky the slit-mask survey never visited is not a control. And the smaller footprint makes randoms overlap *more*, which is the right direction — randoms that overlap less than the data would understate the stack's own bin–bin correlation.

No other spec moved. `bin-correlation-diagnostics.md` (37 days) and `wavelength-zero-point.md` (32 days, still "nothing here has been executed") are the two that should have.

## Literature scan

Checked against all 70 filenames in `docs/literature/` and against the standing suggestions carried in the 07-10 through 08-23 briefings (Arrigoni Battaia 2016, Bennett 2026, Carr 2025, Cerdosino 2025, Claeyssens 2022, Davis 2023, Feldmeier 2013, Firestone 2026, Gagnon-Hartman 2026, Gallego 2021, Gronke 2015, Hall 2026, Kikuchihara 2022, Kikuta 2023 (HSC), Li 2025, Li & Zheng 2025, Lujan Niemeyer 2026, Matsuda 2012, Roy 2026, Runnholm 2023, Saldana-Lopez 2026, Sebastian 2026, Smirnov 2026, Verhamme 2018, Weiss 2024, Wu 2020, Xue 2017, Yu 2025, Zhang 2024 (MAMMOTH), Zhang 2026 (LAMOST), Zhang & Zaritsky 2024 — none re-listed). Weighted toward what recommendation 1 makes urgent: **things the paper will have to cite in a Results or Discussion section**, not further pipeline reading.

**Recent:**

1. **Laferte-Urrutia 2026** — The new era of Lyman alpha emitters (LAEs): Typical star formation histories of LAEs in the ILLUSTRIS simulation (arXiv:2606.25126, June 2026). Traces main-progenitor star formation histories for 6,051 simulated LAEs at z=2.0 in IllustrisTNG-100 and classifies them with k-means, finding **four** classes by time-of-peak-SF — 0.0, 0.5, 0.9 and 1.6 Gyr before observation, at 35%/33%/21%/11% — rather than the three archetypes Firestone et al. found observationally. It is the simulation-side companion to the Firestone 2026 suggestion carried last week, and it matters for `GOALS.md` §6's burst → post-burst → steady ordering in a specific way: the pre-registered sequence has three states and the simulation prefers four, with the two intermediate classes separated by only ~0.4 Gyr. Worth reading before the ordering is written as a prediction, because "how many phases" is a choice VDFI would otherwise be making silently.

2. **Saldana-Lopez 2025** — The Lyman-alpha and Continuum Origins Survey II: the connection between the escape of ionizing radiation and Lyman-alpha halos in star-forming galaxies (arXiv:2504.07074, April 2025; accepted January 2026). Fits Sérsic-plus-exponential 2D profiles to Lyα and UV imaging of 42 star-forming galaxies at z≈0.3 with measured LyC escape fractions (0.01–0.49), finds the **Lyα halo scale length is ~10× the UV on average**, and reports a significant **anti-correlation between f_esc and the Lyα Halo Fraction**, proposing HF as a new LyC indicator. Two direct contacts: that 10:1 ratio is the same number VDFI measures on real CFHT-LS data (inner h1 ≈ 16–17 kpc Lyα vs ≈1.55 kpc UV), from a different instrument, redshift and method; and the f_esc–HF anti-correlation *is* `GOALS.md` §6's Steidel test, already run, in **HF** — the exact quantity §3's glossary defines. ⚠️ The standing-suggestion list carries a "Saldana-Lopez 2026"; confirm that is a different paper before assuming this one is new.

**Older, likely not yet reviewed:**

3. **White 2024** — The clustering of Lyman Alpha Emitting galaxies at z=2–3 (arXiv:2406.01803, June 2024). Measures clustering for ~4,000 ODIN narrow-band-selected LAEs at z=2.45 and 3.1 over 9 deg² **centred on COSMOS**, with DESI spectroscopy constraining the interloper fraction and n(z), and covariances from matched N-body mocks. Reports r₀ = 3.0 ± 0.2 h⁻¹ Mpc at both redshifts and **bias 1.7 (z=2.45) and 2.0 (z=3.1)**. This is the cleanest available external anchor for the b in the ⟨I⟩·b degeneracy — same field, essentially the same redshift, an independently vetted sample, and a bias derived from mocks rather than assumed. It also sits directly beside the r₀ = 3.29/h Bielby cross-correlation value the two-halo chain currently leans on, which makes it a consistency check rather than an import.

4. **Liu 2024** — Prospects for detecting cosmic filaments in Lyman-alpha emission across redshifts z = 2–5 (arXiv:2409.11088, September 2024). Uses TNG50 to compute Lyα surface brightness from non-star-forming gas in cosmic filaments, finds emission is dominated by temperature-sensitive **collisional excitation** (so filaments get *brighter* toward lower redshift, against the naive density scaling), and that >70% of filament emission sits below 10⁻²¹ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² arcsec⁻² — undetectable with 8 m-class instruments, and explicitly notes VIRUS among the relevant facilities. Relevant to the layout's §5 Discussion "Outside" bullet, which lists filamentary emission as one of three candidate origins for the outer term: this is the quantitative case that filaments alone are too faint at the relevant surface brightness, which sharpens the discrimination against Byrohl's "photons from other halos" rather than leaving all three candidates equally weighted.
