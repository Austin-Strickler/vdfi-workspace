## Literature scan

Checked against all 59 filenames in `docs/literature/` and against the standing suggestions carried in the 07-10 / 07-13 / 07-17 / 07-21 / 07-22 / 07-26 briefings (Carr 2025, Cerdosino 2025, Claeyssens 2022, Davis 2023, Gagnon-Hartman 2026, Gallego 2021, Li 2025, Roy 2026, Runnholm 2023, Sebastian 2026, Smirnov 2026, Verhamme 2018, Weiss 2024, Wu 2020, Xue 2017, Yu 2025 — none re-listed). Weighted toward this week's two live problems: **reporting a faint-CGM null honestly**, and **velocity zero-point calibration against an external anchor**.

**Recent:**

1. **Hall 2026** — Mapping the Extended Lyman-Alpha Emission within the Circumgalactic Medium of Quasars Hosted by Dusty Starbursts with CubeCarve (arXiv:2601.22063, January 2026). Introduces CubeCarve, a dual-channel deconvolution that separates unresolved point-source emission from extended structure in IFU cubes, explicitly to recover faint CGM Lyα "without introducing subtraction artifacts." The directly load-bearing part for VDFI is not the deconvolution but the covariance treatment: the paper states that convolution and resampling of IFU data introduce additional covariance that must be accounted for *before* any extended-Lyα extraction, and estimates it empirically — which is the same argument `bin-correlation-diagnostics.md` makes and a published precedent for the random-position-null approach in recommendation 3.

2. **Zhang 2026** — A General Framework for Radial Velocity Calibration in Low-Resolution Spectroscopic Surveys: Correcting Wavelength-Dependent and Global Systematics with Application to LAMOST DR9 (arXiv:2604.19119, April 2026). *(Note: `docs/literature/` already holds a different Zhang 2026 — the CLAMATO cross-correlation paper — so this needs a disambiguating filename.)* Separates two failure modes in a low-resolution fiber survey at R ~ 1800: wavelength-dependent inconsistency between spectral segments, and a global zero-point offset shifting whole spectra, correcting each at spectrograph and fiber level using repeat observations internally plus APOGEE and Gaia RVS externally. Derived zero-point corrections run −2.5 to +10.0 km s⁻¹ and vary with time and spectrograph. This is a methods template for exactly the Gaia-anchored Tier 2 route in `wavelength-zero-point.md`, and its two-mode decomposition is worth borrowing when writing up the air→vacuum result: it gives a published vocabulary for saying which part of a velocity systematic is rigid and which is wavelength-dependent.

**Older, likely not yet reviewed:**

3. **Arrigoni Battaia 2016** — The Stacked Lyman-Alpha Emission Profile from the Circum-Galactic Medium of z~2 Quasars (arXiv:1604.02942, ApJ, April 2016). Stacks 15 deep GMOS narrow-band images, carefully PSF-subtracting the unresolved component and, in their own framing, "paying close attention to sources of systematic error," to reach SB ~10⁻¹⁹ erg s⁻¹ cm⁻² arcsec⁻² at ~200 kpc — reporting a **2.3σ** measurement of (5.5 ± 3.1)×10⁻²⁰ in a 50–500 kpc annulus. Read it for the write-up, not the physics: it is a well-regarded worked example of reporting a marginal/null outer-halo measurement with the sensitivity argument attached, which is precisely the problem recommendation 2 exists to solve.

4. **Zhang & Zaritsky 2024** — A MUSE Source-Blind Survey for Emission from the Circumgalactic Medium (arXiv:2410.05392, October 2024). A source-blind, wide-redshift (z ~ 0–5) narrow-band search of archival MUSE data for CGM emission, cataloguing resolved detections including a 60 kpc Lyα structure at z = 3.9 and a 130 kpc (~R_vir) [O II] feature tracing a galaxy interaction. Relevant as the complement to VDFI's approach: a blind survey's detection threshold *is* its result, so the paper is forced to be explicit about what it could and could not have found — and its resolved velocity fields at ~R_vir scales are a useful external sanity check on the kind of large-scale coherent motion the two-halo reading implies.
