## What's next

**1. Run `flux_scale_check` on real stars. Until it has been run, every flux and luminosity in the project carries an unverified factor of 2.**

The gap is not that a number is wrong — it is that the project cannot currently say which of two numbers is right. `blue_red_side_ratio` now multiplies by Δλ, which is unambiguously the correct operation *if* the cube values are a flux density per Å. If they are per 2-Å pixel, the correct operation is to sum, and the fix has moved the luminosity scale from 5× too high to 2.5× too low. Those two hypotheses differ by a factor of 2 in every absolute quantity the project will report, and nothing on disk distinguishes them: the changelog says so in its own words, and the file headers do not answer it.

`starpsf.flux_scale_check` was written on 09-17 to answer exactly this. It rebuilds each star's total flux the way the galaxy surface brightness is built — average-fiber value × A_ann/A_fib, divided by the Moffat curve of growth from `psfmodel` — synthesizes a PS1 g magnitude, and compares against PS1 DR2. **R ≈ 1 means per Å; R ≈ 2 means per 2 Å.** On synthetic stars it recovers 1.000 and 2.001. It has never touched data.

Why now: it is the cheapest decisive item in the project, and it gates more than the unit. Because it divides out the Moffat curve of growth from `psfmodel`, a clean R also independently validates the 2.090″ effective PSF and the aperture/area bookkeeping — which is why the function reports R against aperture as well as against magnitude, colour and field. A drift with aperture means the aperture correction or the area bookkeeping is wrong, not the unit; that is a second measurement for free, on the calibration recommendation 2 depends on.

**One dependency to settle before running it, and it is the same one flagged last week.** `TODO.md` still carries *"Re-extract the stars under the 80–90″ background config"* unchecked, with the note that `0_Extractor_Stars` runs are on the 57–63″ setup, while `psf-model.md` §1 states the stars were extracted with the galaxies' background annulus. One of those is wrong and it matters here specifically: a background error is *additive*, so it does not divide out of a flux ratio and shows up as an R that drifts with magnitude — the same signature `flux_scale_check`'s magnitude diagnostic is meant to read as saturation or non-linearity. Running the test on stars extracted under the wrong annulus risks getting a clean-looking R for the wrong reason, or a magnitude trend attributed to the wrong cause. Two minutes on the run config, first.

## Literature scan

Checked against all 72 filenames in `docs/literature/` and against the standing suggestions carried in the 07-10 through 09-13 briefings (Arrigoni Battaia 2016, Bennett 2026, Carr 2025, Cerdosino 2025, Claeyssens 2022, Davis 2023, Dong 2024, Feldmeier 2013, Firestone 2026, Gagnon-Hartman 2026, Gallego 2021, Gronke 2015, Guo 2023, Hall 2026, Infante-Sainz 2020, Kikuchihara 2022, Kikuta 2023, Kozlova 2026, Laferte-Urrutia 2026, Laishram 2026, Li 2025, Li & Zheng 2025, Liu 2022, Liu 2024, Lujan Niemeyer 2026, Matsuda 2012, Mentuch Cooper 2026, Mirza Khanlari 2025, Momose 2014, Pharo 2024, Roy 2026, Runnholm 2023, Saldana-Lopez 2025, Sebastian 2026, Smirnov 2026, Stephan 2024, Verhamme 2018, Weiss 2024, Weiss 2025, White 2024, Wu 2020, Xue 2017, Yu 2025, Zhang 2024, Zhang 2026 — none re-listed). **Weighted toward the absolute flux scale and the halo-mass chain this week**, because those are the two places recommendations 1 and 2 need an external number rather than an internal one.

**Recent:**

1. **Xiao 2026** — A Large and Precise All-Sky Photometric Standard Star Dataset Across More Than 200 Passbands (arXiv:2601.05486, January 2026). Builds the BEst STars database (BEST): >200 million standards across >200 passbands at better than 10 mmag, constructed from Gaia XP spectrophotometry re-calibrated against LAMOST and corrected for position-dependent systematics. This is directly operational for recommendation 1 — `flux_scale_check` currently queries PS1 DR2 g through VizieR, and PS1's own calibration is known to carry position-dependent errors at the 5–7 mmag level before correction, which is a floor on how tightly the R ≈ 1 vs R ≈ 2 test can be quoted. More useful than the precision, though, is that BEST supplies synthetic magnitudes in arbitrary passbands from the same stars: the check could be run in *several* bands across the VIRUS range rather than g alone, which turns a single scale factor into a scale factor with a wavelength dependence — i.e. it would test the throughput curve, not just the unit. Worth reading before the reference photometry is fixed, not after.

2. **Ebina 2026** — The 3D clustering of Lyman Alpha Emitters measured with DESI (arXiv:2606.24852, June 2026). Measures the two-point correlation function and power spectrum of DESI-spectroscopic LAEs selected from Blanco/DECam IBIS, including cross-correlations with DESI quasars, and derives a linear bias **b ≈ 2.31–2.62 over 2.26 < z < 3.41** from both analytic and HOD modelling — with the HOD fits explicitly characterising the LAE–halo connection and constraining how much of the clustering signal is radiative-transfer contamination rather than halo occupation. Two contacts with VDFI. First, the 08-31 `VDFI Notes.txt` derivation left the diffuse two-halo interpretation stuck on the degenerate product ⟨I⟩·b; this is a modern, spectroscopic, cosmic-noon measurement of the *b* half with error bars, on a redshift range that overlaps the sample almost exactly. Second, and more immediately, the HOD analysis is a live external constraint on the LAE mass–halo mapping at the same epoch where `smhm_scatter_dex = 0.2` is currently a placeholder — different selection, so not a drop-in, but it is the kind of number a referee will expect the 0.2 to have been checked against. Caveat worth stating: the radiative-transfer term they fit is precisely the thing that makes an LAE bias not transferable to a continuum-selected sample without argument.

**Older, likely not yet reviewed:**

3. **Matthee 2016** — The CALYMHA survey: Lyα escape fraction and its dependence on galaxy properties at z = 2.23 (arXiv:1602.02756, February 2016; MNRAS 458, 449). Measures Lyα for 488 **Hα-selected** galaxies at z = 2.23 with a custom narrow-band filter matched to the HiZELS Hα volume, and reports a median stacked escape fraction of **1.6 ± 0.5%** after dust-correcting Hα (4.0 ± 1.0% without), with individual star-forming galaxies spanning 2–30%. This is the closest published analogue to the measurement `GOALS.md` §6 specifies — absolute f_esc from a Balmer-line intrinsic budget on a continuum-ish-selected, non-Lyα-selected sample at almost exactly VDFI's low-z half — and it is not in `docs/literature/`, which holds Hayes 2010/2011 and Weiss 2021's descendants but nothing from the CALYMHA line. Its practical use this week is as an order-of-magnitude arbiter for recommendation 1: a project-level f_esc coming out near a few percent is consistent with the published z ~ 2 picture, while one coming out near 10% or near 0.5% would be the 5×-vs-2× ambiguity showing up as a physical implausibility. That is a weak check, not a proof — but it is free, and it is the check a reader will make in their head anyway.

4. **Gu 2016** — Hierarchical Galaxy Growth and Scatter in the Stellar Mass–Halo Mass Relation (arXiv:1602.01099, February 2016; ApJ 833, 2). Models the in-situ buildup of stellar mass and shows that an intrinsic scatter of **0.2 dex in the growth channel** reproduces the observed scatter–halo-mass relation from 10¹² to 10¹⁴·⁷⁵ M⊙, and — the part that matters for the placeholder — derives how that scatter *varies*, declining from ~0.25 dex at M ≈ 10¹¹ M⊙ to ~0.12 dex at M ≈ 10¹³, and increasing with redshift. Listed because `stack.sample_virial_radius_kpc`'s `smhm_scatter_dex = 0.2` is currently an unsourced constant that, as of 09-16, propagates into the R_vir band and therefore into Paper I's headline comparison. Gu's value is scatter in log M\* at fixed M_h while the code applies scatter in log M_h at fixed M\*; those differ by the local slope of the relation, so this is a reference to *convert* rather than to copy — which is itself the point, since the conversion is the thing that should be written into Methods rather than a bare 0.2. Note also that the mass- and redshift-dependence means a single scalar is an approximation whose direction is known: it under-disperses the low-mass end of the sample.
